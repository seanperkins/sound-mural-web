How it works

It's watching the waveform, not a clock

Most visualizers are an animation with a volume knob attached. SoundMural measures the audio sixty times a second — bass, mid, treble, transients, the beat, and how hard the track is building — and hands each of those to a different part of the picture.

◆

A fractal that can genuinely break

A Julia set is connected if and only if its constant lies inside the Mandelbrot set. Bass energy pushes that constant toward the boundary, so the mural strains apart under heavy low end, shatters into disconnected dust on a real drop, then reforms. The mathematics supplies the drama; the mapping only decides when to cross the line.

♫

Plays the library you already own

Point it at a Plex, Jellyfin, or Navidrome server, browse by album art, and stream or download. Those three hand the visualizer real PCM samples — an eight-band spectrum, onsets, beat, and phrase tension. Spotify connects for now-playing, artwork, and transport.

◐

The colour comes off the cover

Every visualization samples a colour ramp, and that ramp can be lifted from the artwork of whatever is playing. Put on a record with a red sleeve and the room goes red — without any visualization knowing where the palette came from.

▣

One camera over all twenty-six

Every visualization sits under a shared camera rig: it pans along a Lissajous path that never quite repeats, pushes in on bass, and rolls on the beat. The frame moves, not just the pattern inside it — which is what stops any of them reading as a loop.

◎

Every knob is yours

Each visualization exposes its own live settings — speed, reactivity, and two parameters particular to it. Lock the ones you like, randomize the rest, or reset. Reduce Motion damps the camera drift and pace without ever muting the music's authority over the picture.

◯

Offline, and private by design

Anything you download plays with no network at all. Connections to your server are TLS-first, nothing you play is uploaded, and the app collects no telemetry and tracks no data. There is no SoundMural account, because there is no SoundMural server.

Questions

Everything you might ask

When can I actually get it?
Not yet — SoundMural is finished enough to demo and not yet on the App Store. The visuals, the camera rig, and the Plex / Jellyfin / Navidrome / Spotify players are all built and running on real hardware; the free-versus-paid split is the remaining piece. Leave your address and you'll get one email on the day it ships, and nothing else.
Are the clips on this page really the app?
Yes. Each one is rendered by the app's own Metal shaders, driven by the same feature pipeline an Apple TV uses — a real track analysed for its eight-band spectrum, onsets, beat and phrase tension, then handed frame by frame to the same mappers. Nothing here is a mock-up or a browser imitation. What the clips don't show is scale: the app runs 1080p60 on your TV, takes its palette from your album art, and doesn't loop after five seconds.
Which Apple TVs does it run on?
SoundMural needs tvOS 26 and is tuned for Apple TV 4K (3rd generation), where it holds 1080p60 with adaptive quality — the expensive visualizations trade detail rather than frames. If your Apple TV can't run tvOS 26, it won't install.
Which music sources does it support, and does it matter which?
It matters a great deal. Plex, Jellyfin and Navidrome stream audio the app can actually analyse, giving the visualizer all twelve measured features — eight-band spectrum, onsets, beat, and the tension that builds across a phrase. Spotify works for browsing, now-playing, artwork and transport, but DRM means no app may touch its audio, so those visuals run on a musical driver derived from the track's own metadata rather than real samples.
Do I need an internet connection?
No. Anything you download to the TV plays fully offline. Streaming works anywhere your own server is reachable — usually your home network. SoundMural has no cloud service of its own to be offline from.
Does it collect or share any of my data?
No. Connections to your music server are TLS-first, nothing you play is uploaded, and the app collects no telemetry and tracks no data. There's no account to make. Spotify uses the system's standard browser sign-in, exactly like Apple's own Music app, and the token stays on the device.
Can I change how a visualization behaves?
Yes. Each one carries its own live knobs — speed, reactivity, and two parameters specific to it — adjustable while it's on screen. Lock the values you've settled on, randomize everything else, or reset to defaults. Your settings persist per visualization, so they survive switching away and back.
I'm sensitive to motion. Is this watchable?
The system Reduce Motion setting is honoured throughout, and it does something specific: it damps the non-audio motion — camera drift, pan, roll, and pace — while leaving the music's own influence on the picture intact. You still see the track; you just aren't flown around it. Metaballs, Aurora and Plasma are the gentlest of the twenty-six even without it. Every clip on this page also stays paused on a still frame if your browser reports Reduce Motion.
Is there really no subscription?
Correct. Free to try, and a single $9.99 one-time purchase unlocks every visualization in all five packs, including future ones, on every Apple TV on your account. No recurring charge, ever — the app has no server to pay for.
